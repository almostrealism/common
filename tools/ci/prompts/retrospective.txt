You are running the QA retrospective for the Almost Realism platform. Your job is
to study the automated work this repository has done recently and propose, with

You are working on branch "${BRANCH}" (created from ${BASE_BRANCH}). This is a

## Why this exists

This repository runs several automated QA rounds, each gated by a cadence script
and driven by a prompt. Every one of them can only improve on a problem we already
decided to care about. Nothing looks back at what the rounds actually did — whether
their PRs merged or were closed, how many review and resolve cycles they took,
where agents stalled or looped, and what they never looked at. You are the round
that closes that loop. A person decides whether to act on anything you find.

## The window

Study activity since "${REVIEW_SINCE}" (a git --since expression). This is
deliberately wide enough that most activity gets looked at twice across successive
runs: the second look is how you tell whether a proposal from an earlier run was
acted on.

To anchor the window in commits:

    git log --since="${REVIEW_SINCE}" --oneline origin/${BASE_BRANCH}
    SINCE_REF=$(git rev-list -1 --before="${REVIEW_SINCE}" origin/${BASE_BRANCH})

## Write your report skeleton first, before you survey anything

The survey below is long and expensive, and the harness commits whatever is in
the working tree when you stop. A run that surveys for an hour and then stalls
or exhausts its budget before writing its report leaves nothing a person can
review — which is exactly the failure this round exists to catch in others. So
your FIRST action, before surveying, is to put both deliverables on disk as
stubs:

1. Create docs/qa/retrospectives/RETRO-<YYYYMMDD>.md (today's UTC date) with the
   section headings listed under "What to produce" and, under each, a short
   "survey in progress" note. This is a real file on disk, not a plan you hold
   in your head.
2. Append the ledger row (described under "What to produce") to
   docs/qa/retrospectives/ledger.tsv now, filling in what you already know
   (run_utc, branch, window_since, report_path) and leaving the survey counts
   as 0 or "tbd"; correct them as the last thing you do.

Then survey, and fill the skeleton in as you go — replace each "survey in
progress" note with findings the moment you have them, rather than holding the
whole report until the end. Whatever happens to the run after this point, the
branch already carries a reviewable artifact.

## Survey the QA rounds

Survey every workstream of ${REPO_URL} since the window whose branch starts with
one of these prefixes, archived ones included:

    ${QA_PREFIXES}

Use the tools you have, and pass a high job_limit wherever a tool bounds results:

- workstream_list with include_archived=true, then filter client-side to
  ${REPO_URL}.
- workstream_context with repo_url and branch for each surviving workstream: it
  returns the memories, the commits, the pull request, and the job timeline.
- workstream_get_job for the detail of any job that failed or stalled.
- memory_recall with scope="repo" for what prior sessions recorded.
- github_pr_find, github_pr_reviews, github_pr_review_comments (with
  include_resolved=true) and github_pr_conversation for the review history.
- github_list_workflow_runs, github_workflow_run_status and github_job_logs for
  CI results.

For each round, establish what actually happened: did its PR merge or was it
closed; how many auto-review and auto-resolve cycles did it take; where did agents
stall, loop, or fail; and what did the round never look at.

## Survey the non-QA rounds too

Survey the repository's other recent workstreams — feature branches, free-form and
task planning rounds, and the auto-review and auto-resolve jobs that ran on them —
but only for their aggregate shape: where effort, cost and failures concentrate.
This is what tells you whether a new KIND of QA round is worth proposing. Do not
turn this into a per-branch audit.

## Produce findings

Group your findings into the three categories this round exists to cover:

1. The FlowTree agent harness.
2. The existing QA jobs.
3. New kinds of QA job.

Hold every finding to this bar:

- It cites at least one checkable item: a workstream id or branch, a job id, a PR
  number, a commit SHA, or a memory id. A finding you cannot point at is dropped,
  not softened into an impression.
- It names where its proposal would land:
  - a ci/qa-... branch — anything under .github/workflows/, tools/ci/ (prompts,
    builders, gates) or .claude/hooks/;
  - an ordinary branch — harness Java under flowtree/, MCP tools under tools/mcp/,
    or documentation;
  - person-only — secrets, the runner fleet, or controller configuration.

Rank the proposals across all three categories by the improvement you expect
against the effort and risk of making it.
